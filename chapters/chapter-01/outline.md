## 2. Character knowledge and concerns

**Anna (34).** *Knows:* the published anomalies the initiative is chasing (river purity, odd low-frequency readings in old seismic records, the regional health statistics), Deka's public framing, Antonov's claims and fate, and that he worked at this station. *Doesn't know:* the Resonator, Shulganite, Deka's resource objective, Timur's find, Zamir's models, anything about strain or failure. *On her mind, plot aside:* she's sleep-deprived; this job is a career gamble she took after years of keeping Antonov's name off her CV; she's an outsider (Belarusian, Russian-speaking, in a Russian-led international operation run by a corporation); she arrives into a camp where everyone else already has their routines and their jokes. *Temperament:* contained, observant, stubborn. She watches moving water when she thinks. She distrusts performance and charisma.

**Sokolov (Deka security liaison).** *Knows:* the security plan; more about Deka's interest than he'll ever say. *Concern:* control, protocol, people who wander. *Manner:* minimal, observant, not cruel; "Dr. Morozova." He gives away nothing about the real objective.

**Zamir (glimpsed).** Seen working alone, outside the briefing. A wave-1 tech's offhand remark shows the camp's attitude to him. They don't meet properly. That's Ch. 3.

**Wave-1 crews.** Tired, proprietary, a little contemptuous of the newly arrived "scientists." That friction is the team tension.

**Eva Reinhardt.** Not on the page. Her name is on the welcome documents and signatures (writer's choice between "Eva or Sokolov": Sokolov in person, since an arrival day runs through security. *Author may override.*)

## 3. Must NOT reveal or explain
- No Resonator, Shulganite, Vodyanoy, Humming Stone, or Lampshell discoveries (Ben's chapters).
- Deka's real objective. Show **secrecy** only: restricted zones, a data gap, rules. Never a reason.
- Nothing about strain, failure, or healing as a cause. The health statistics, if they appear, are **one unremarked line** in the data pack (CM8: never stated).
- No link to Timur (CH1-2).
- Antonov's claims: Anna won't let herself finish the thought. No exposition of his theory beyond one line of what he was mocked for.

## 4. Proposed scenes (about 4,500 words)

**S1 — The approach (about 1,000).** The wave-2 transport up the valley, Anna by the window watching the river (her habit). The new arrivals' small talk washes around her. The first sight of the station: weathered Soviet concrete (a gauge house, a weir, rows of empty windows) with Deka's new prefab units and generators bolted onto it. She knows Antonov worked here. One thought, kept to herself. *Dramatic question:* what has she let herself in for? *Texture:* motion sickness, a colleague's bad joke, the river glittering ordinarily. Hands off into the gate.

**S2 — Intake (about 1,300).** Badges, phone rules, an NDA, a camp map with zones shaded "restricted" and no explanation. Sokolov runs it: courteous, unreadable, "Dr. Morozova." Wave-1 staff treat the arrivals like tourists. The data pack: wave 1's baseline monitoring of the Shulgan. Anna reads it the way she reads everything, for what's missing. Some monitoring points are there, some are blank, and no one will say why (secrecy). The health statistics are one line she notes and passes. *Dramatic question:* what is this operation actually for? (She can't answer it, and shouldn't.)

**S3 — The consultant (about 700).** Walking her kit to her quarters, she sees a man alone at the old gauge house with a notebook, outside the briefing everyone else is at. A wave-1 tech: the local consultant, don't get him started. She notices the camp's whole posture toward him. He looks up, and that's all. (Zamir, glimpsed.)

**S4 — The river (about 1,500).** Evening. She goes down to the Shulgan, to the edge of the permitted area or a step past it, with her own battered meter, because she trusts her instrument over the data pack. The river in late summer looks like any clear mountain river. **What's wrong only shows in the reading**, and in what her hands remember of water from home. Told in plain terms (lay-accessible): water that has run through a mountain of limestone carrying none of it. The Polesia memory: water is never still, even when it looks it. Antonov's old claim surfaces and she shuts it down. *Ending (concrete, no button):* she writes the reading in her own notebook and **doesn't enter it into Deka's system**. It's a small private act that starts her arc toward secrecy without anyone remarking on it.

## 5. Pacing
S1 eases in. S2 is busy and procedural, with friction in it. S3 is a quiet hinge. S4 slows to one person and a river, and the chapter's weight lands there. The total of about 4,500 sits mid-range. S4 earns the most space because it carries the core mystery and Anna's interior.

## 7. Plausibility notes
1. In late summer, karst rivers often run **visually clear**, so the anomaly can't be "it looks clear." It has to be what the reading says: water from limestone that carries none of the limestone. That keeps it consistent with the prologue's chemistry and stays lay-readable.
2. Ufa to the Shulgan basin is about five hours by road. A sizable operation would run convoys, maybe a helicopter. A Deka bus or convoy fits "wave 2."
3. Old Soviet hydrology stations: gauge houses, weirs, cableways for flow measurement. A few visible pieces, no inventory.
