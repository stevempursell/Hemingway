# Editorial Review — Prologue Draft v1

**Reviewed:** 2026-10-14. `draft-v1.md`, **5,844 words** (target about 5,100; +15%).
**Method note:** done in the same session as the drafting, not in a fresh context. That limits independence. The echo check and the pattern counts below are mechanical (grep/awk) to compensate. A fresh-context review can be run on request.

---

## 1. Character authenticity and dialogue: Adequate

- **Alina is the draft's best-realized person.** Her concerns are her own (the cake belted into the back seat, the grandmother's party, the field hours), and her fear shows as behavior rather than statements: she goes quiet, doesn't come closer, counts the minutes. Protect her.
- **But in s01 nearly every Alina line is a quip.** The onions run, the Deka "community engagement" joke, the fisherman, "because she was an idiot": four comic runs before the anomaly. Each is fine on its own. Together they tip into sitcom rhythm at the very moment a tense cold open needs to start tightening. *Fix:* cut or fold one of the runs (the fisherman exchange is the most expendable), and let one joke fall flat.
- **Zamir drifts toward the wise-mentor register.** "You wrote things down." (s03) is a neat recognition beat. "Even what seems stupid. Especially that. By tomorrow you'll have started tidying it." is a polished aphorism, the kind of line the brief warns against. "Where," with no question mark, and "It was impossible to tell what kind of hm it was," make him coy on the narrator's behalf. The bible's Zamir is *dryly humorous*, and here he has no humor at all. *Fix:* keep the questions, which are the best part (did it mix, was the cold in the water or the stone). Cut "Especially that…tidying it." Give him one plain, dry, human line (about the hour, the thaw, or Timur's going in) in place of the mystique.
- "'That wasn't happening,' Timur said, stupidly." The adverb is a narrator judgment. And see §7: the line is an echo.

## 2. Scene development and pacing: Adequate

- These are real scenes, not a synopsis. Procedure, banter, and physical struggle are all on the page. The crawl and the trap (s02) are the strongest stretch: continuous, physical, escalating.
- **s01 front-loads background.** Paragraphs 3–6 (thesis and Sibay, the walk-in, permits, Deka scholarship) run about 450 words of context before the anomaly appears at about the 900-word mark. Each paragraph is in voice, but stacked together they read as an establishing block. *Fix:* keep the walk-in images (shot-up sign, birches in the sinkholes) and interleave a sentence or two of thesis and permit context into the action. Cut the rest.
- **s03 is slow in the wrong place.** The dread builds through data, as planned, but the data passages are long (see §3/§6). The best beats in s03 are human and physical (Alina's texts, the scar, the call, the vial), and they're waiting behind the numbers.
- The cut from the cave to the lab works. Alina's texts do the bridge's job in about 80 words.

## 3. Exposition and premature revelation: Needs work

- **No bible leakage.** No Resonator, Shulganite, Vodyanoy, or Deka motive. Zamir explains nothing. Good.
- **Two agency flickers where the plan allowed one.** (a) s02: the stone "had shifted its weight, the way a large animal shifts in its sleep." (b) s03: "as if someone had run the corrections on the water itself." "Someone" implies a designer. That's the series' central mystery, nudged on page six. *Fix:* in s03 make it agentless ("as if the corrections had been run on the water itself"), or keep only one of the two flickers.
- **Justification creep** (the author's 2026-10-14 note):
  - s03: "That should not have lasted. Water that pure doesn't stay pure. It takes carbon dioxide out of the air…He knew this the way he knew his own phone number." The paragraph exists to explain why the reading is wrong. *Fix:* one clause, or show it (he waits for the number to climb, and it doesn't).
  - s03 isotope paragraph (about 230 words): method detail (six injections, the standard, scatter) at report density. This is exactly "book report." *Fix:* keep three gut facts. It's as clean as the ultrapure tap. There's no limestone in limestone water. The two vials are identical, and the standard isn't. Cut to about 120 words.
  - s02 quartz/flowstone reasoning (about 130 words): natural for a chemist, but it reads like a field-notes entry. *Fix:* keep the boiled-milk image and one reason it isn't quartz. Cut the flowstone argument.
  - s01 "Karst water in a thaw should have been the color of weak tea…": short and earned. Keep.

## 5. Prose rhythm and readability: Adequate

- **Strongest sentences:** the clear ribbon "holding together the way a dye band holds in a chromatography column, until the current got its fingers in and smeared it away." The birches "patiently waiting for the water to leave." "For a while she didn't say anything, which for Alina was a considerable statement." The data-correction idea in s03. "He set it down again exactly where it had been, square on its paper towel, as though that mattered."
- **Weakest:** "He knew a little mineralogy, which was enough to know that nothing he knew looked like this." (knew/know ×3, and a button). "It was impossible to tell what kind of hm it was." (coy). "'That wasn't happening,' Timur said, stupidly." (echo plus adverb).
- Sentence length varies well. Long cumulative sentences carry the crawl. Fragments are rare.

## 6. Scientific and physical plausibility: Strong

- Conductivity near the ultrapure value and not drifting up: uncanny without breaking physics. ✓
- Calcium indistinguishable from the blank, in karst water: the right chemist's alarm. ✓
- Isotope identity beyond instrument precision, against a normally scattering standard, with no age claim: ✓ per P4.
- Ears popping on a pressure surge, current reversal, line markers swinging crosswise: physically coherent. ✓
- Timing: site to Ufa in about 5 hours, lab results around 23:30, the call at 00:40, fans off at 01:00. Consistent. ✓
- Nit: the "calcium channel" on the field probe (s02) is never set up. Plausible (ion-selective probes exist), but it's one more instrument noun.

## 7. Mechanical and AI patterns: Needs work

**Echoes of the rejected AI prologue** (checked mechanically against `source/existing-prose/`). The author ruled that text out (P1). These are the most serious problem in the draft:

| Draft v1 | AI Google Doc |
|---|---|
| "Take your sample, and let's go." (s02) | "Take your sample…and let's leave." |
| "You felt that," she said. (s02) | "You felt that too." |
| "That wasn't happening," Timur said (s02) | "That wasn't happening before," Timur said |
| "Moisture." / "Probably moisture." (s01) | "Probably moisture in the contacts." |
| limestone meeting the seam "as though one had grown around the other" (s02) | "as though the rock had formed around it rather than through it" |
| stone shifting "the way a large animal shifts in its sleep" (s02) | "something enormous shifting in uneasy sleep" |

*Fix:* rewrite all six from scratch, with different beats, not paraphrases. The moisture exchange is the most generic and could survive in a different form. The other five should go.

**Pattern counts** (5,844 words):
- "the way …" simile construction: **15** (2.6 per 1,000). A tic. Cut to 5 or fewer.
- "He found that…": **3**. Cut to 1.
- Reflexive button endings: "nothing he knew looked like this"; "There was no mist."; "pointing at the walls, pointing nowhere." (also a doublet); "colder than anything else he was carrying"; "He found that he didn't like looking at it." Five. Keep at most one.
- Short standalone narrative paragraphs: 9. Six are legitimate turns. Three are clipped-intensity devices: "It read zero point zero six." "He sat back on the stool." "It was twenty to one." Fold them into neighboring paragraphs.
- "It was not a sound." (s02): a "not X" construction. Rephrase.
- Similes from Timur's world (chromatography, boiled-milk skin, interference pattern, data correction, coat repair): **good**, per the voice profile. Generic ones to replace: "steady as a fan," "the way a large animal."

## Top 3 problems
1. **Echoes of the rejected AI draft:** four dialogue lines and two images (§7). Rewrite all six fresh.
2. **"Book report" density and front-loading:** s03's conductivity and isotope passages, s02's mineral reasoning, s01's background block. Compress. That also brings the length back toward target (about −600 words).
3. **Mechanical tics and the Zamir register:** "the way" ×15, five button endings, three clipped one-liners, two agency flickers. Zamir as sage rather than as a dry, ordinary man.

## What to protect
- Alina: the cake, her quiet in the chamber, "it was twelve minutes. I counted."
- The line-arrows payoff (clipped on with no explanation, then swinging crosswise) and the continuous physicality of the escape.
- Timur's trade-world figurative language, the data-correction idea (made agentless), and the ending: he writes everything down because he was told to, and the time lands as a plain fact.

## Does it read like a novel?
Mostly, yes. s02 reads like a novel. Its long sentences carry the reader through the crawl, and the fear lives in bodies and procedure rather than adjectives. s01 reads like a novel that's still clearing its throat, and s03 slips toward a well-written methods section until the scar and the phone call pull it back. The draft's biggest liability is the AI echoes. They're the clearest sign that the prose leaned on what was in the room instead of on the characters. They're also the easiest thing to fix.
