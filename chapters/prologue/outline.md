## 2. Character knowledge and concerns

**Timur (26).** *Knows:* trace-mineral chemistry; the contamination signatures of this district's abandoned Soviet works (it's his thesis); that his equipment is old and unreliable; that Deka pays for his scholarship and expects reports; enough basic caving from field courses to read airflow and lay a line. Zamir's lectures taught him to look at what others ignore. That shows up as **habit** (checking where cold air comes from, writing down what doesn't fit), not as belief in folklore. *Doesn't know:* anything about a Resonator, Shulganite, a planetary system, or why this water matters beyond being chemically wrong. He has no category for what he's seeing, so his mind goes to equipment error, contamination, a mineral spring. *On his mind, plot aside:* a thesis chapter that's late; the scholarship renewal and the dull Deka quarterly report; money (fuel, the probe's cracked housing, his secondhand raincoat); being taken seriously by his department. *Wants:* data no one else has. That ambition is why he goes in.

**Alina.** *Knows:* the same coursework; that this outing is off the books; that spring flood channels kill people every year. *Doesn't know:* why Timur is really out here beyond "sampling." *On her mind:* her certification hours; getting back to Ufa tonight (P3 proposes an ordinary reason); whether her car will survive the forestry road; being cold, wet, and hungry. *Relation to Timur:* longtime classmate and friend. Their bickering is habit and affection. **No romance** (none in canon; I'm not inventing one).

**Zamir (by phone, late at night).** *Knows:* decades of correlating folklore, seismic records, and hydrological anomalies in this region. What Timur describes will mean far more to him than he lets on. *Behavior:* soft-spoken, dry, asks questions rather than explaining. Patient. Not alarmed in a way Timur can read. *Doesn't do:* warn about Deka, explain the Vodyanoy or the Humming Stone, or name anything.

## 3. What this prologue must NOT reveal or explain
- The Resonator, Shulganite, Deka's real interest, the Vodyanoy as a term, the Humming Stone, the circulatory-organ idea.
- Why Zamir matters to the series. He's a lecturer Timur respects and calls once.
- Any explanation of the purity, the vibration, or the scar. Observations stay observations.
- No character speculates about alien, ancient, or engineered origins. Timur's darkest thought stays within what a chemist would think, plus one irrational flicker at most.
- No foreshadowing chatter about the future (expeditions, companies coming, "someone should know about this").

## 4. Proposed scenes

**Scene 1 — The channel (Part I) — about 1,500 words**
Forestry-road pull-off, then the walk-in past a sagging warning fence and old survey markers to a limestone cut where runoff vanishes under a rusted Soviet-era overhang. Rain, noise, bad footing. Texture: Alina's car and its suspension, a thermos, Timur's probe with its taped housing, his notebook (used, not spotlighted). The bickering (her hours, his idea, the fact that they're not supposed to be here; see P8). Routine sampling procedure, done competently and a bit carelessly. He spots the clear tributary holding its line inside the gray flood. Samples it. The meter glitches and resets, and he shrugs it off as moisture. Alina wants out before the flow rises. He feels cold air pushing out of the collapse and understands what that means. *Dramatic question:* will he go in? *Handoff:* he ties off a line. She follows, complaining.
Zamir's influence: one natural beat. Timur checks the airflow the way a lecturer taught him, or Alina needles him about his notebook. One mention, not a conversation about folklore.

**Scene 2 — The restriction and the chamber (Part II) — about 1,900 words**
The crawl: dragging the sample case, the line paying out, chest-deep cold water, a low airspace. Sound drops away; the water clears. The chamber: lamps on wet black stone; the pale seam beneath clear moving water; the cold coming off it. Timur's chemist's eye tries quartz, calcite, flowstone, and none of them fits. The probe goes wild. A low vibration he feels before he hears; ripples cross in regular lines. Then the trap, physical and procedural: pressure changes (ears), the inlet surges, the current reverses for a second, the line markers swing sideways, Alina goes down and recovers. The vibration hits his chest. Retreat along the line against shifting flow. Out into rain. The tributary is gray again. *Dramatic question:* will they get out? *Texture:* procedure under fear; the dry humor shrinking as things get worse; Alina's shoulder.

**Bridge — The drive back — about 400 words** (see P6)
Continuous, not a time skip. Alina drives. The heater roars. Adrenaline turns silly and then quiet. The cooler of samples between his boots. Her shoulder is bruised and she won't discuss it. She drops him at the chemistry building at night. Ordinary life closing back over the event.

**Scene 3 — The lab (Part III) — about 1,700 words**
After hours: the night watchman, the dead vending machine, the ventilation's rattle, the fluorescent buzz. He runs what he can tonight (see P4): conductivity and TDS; ion chromatography or ICP for trace metals; laser isotope analysis. While runs queue, he waits, eats, writes up field notes, avoids the Deka report. Results: the water is cleaner than the lab's own ultrapure reference blank; none of the contaminants his thesis has documented in this district for a year; two samples, taken minutes and meters apart, matching beyond the instrument's precision. Not filtered. **Corrected**: he reaches for the idea in his own terms. Washing up, he notices the scar (P5). He calls Zamir (P9). Zamir asks questions and says little. After the call: the unplugged field spectrometer on the bench emits a low tone; the sealed vial trembles. Close on an original final image, concrete and understated (P2).

**Total target: about 5,500 words (range 4,800–6,000).**

## 5. Pacing
Scene 1 starts at a working pace (banter, procedure) and tightens with the airflow. Scene 2 accelerates steadily. The trap is the peak, told in longer, breathless sentences rather than one-line paragraphs. The bridge is the breath. Scene 3 is slow and procedural; dread builds through data, not events, then two small physical events and a quiet end.
*Why about 5,500:* the beat sheet has three distinct movements, each with real dramatic material. The existing Google Doc covers Parts I–II in 1,721 words, which by the brief's standard compresses scenes that deserve room (the crawl and the trap especially). A cold open shouldn't overstay its welcome either; this sits at the top of a normal chapter's range.

## 7. Plausibility notes
- **Geography:** Shulgan-Tash (Kapova Cave) is in Burzyansky District, roughly 5 hours by road south of Ufa (I'll verify the exact distance). That supports a late-evening lab: leave the site mid-afternoon, arrive at night. The area is a protected nature reserve, which gives a real reason the trip is "unofficial" (P8).
- **Cold water:** chest-deep April meltwater is a few degrees above freezing. Unprotected, it's incapacitating within minutes. They need neoprene for credibility (P10).
- **Analyses in one night:** conductivity/TDS (minutes), ion chromatography or ICP-MS (an hour or two), and laser water-isotope analysis (δ¹⁸O/δ²H, minutes per injection) are all plausible for a university hydrochemistry lab. **Age dating (e.g. tritium) is not:** it takes weeks. So "isotopic consistency inconsistent with geological age" is better shown as a signature that matches no local water source and is impossibly uniform between samples. See P4.
- **The scar:** your Resonant notes say biological effects are "subtle and statistical… no instant healing." A *dramatic* fade within hours collides with that. See P5.
- **The disconnected spectrometer:** if it's unplugged with its battery out, any sound is uncanny. If the battery's in, a speaker or piezo buzzer is plausible. The vial trembling on the bench reads as sympathetic vibration. No overt horror.
