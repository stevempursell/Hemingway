## A. Consistent across his work (high confidence, use these)

### 1. Dry understatement attached to pain
His most consistent move, across 13 years of samples. An earnest or painful sentence is followed by a short, flat, deflating one. The emotion lands *because* the narration refuses to milk it.
- Evidence: the outcome Witte "knew from experience" (Dust); "Nobody said thank you. Didn't need it." (Durvek); "Make that a solid six." (Palace, comic version); Witte hoping his late wife would be proud of how the house looks.
- **Glubina application:** this is the right register for fear in the caves. Characters undercut their own alarm. Ben's "funny under stress" and Zamir's "dryly humorous" are both natural fits.
- **Caution:** it works because it's occasional. More than once or twice a scene and it becomes a tic. See the reflexive-ending rule in `narrative-principles.md`.

### 2. People defined by work, money, and making do
Economic precarity drives his characters: thin purses, debts, the cold forge, student loans, layoffs, a job "with vague requirements." Competence is shown through improvised repair (the bellows fixed with tar and scrap canvas) and the texture of labor (landscaping, dishes, scrap heaps).
- **Glubina application:** Timur is "underfunded" and on a Deka scholarship. Money, bad equipment, and institutional dependence should be lived detail, not commentary. Ben is "always repairing something." Ground scenes in the physical work being done.

### 3. Grief and feeling through objects and routine, not declaration
Feeling arrives through things: the plates washed seven times a week, the forge's hollow sound before a funeral, Grandad's crude joke and the line that he misses it. He rarely names an emotion head-on. When he does, it's brief and plain.
- **Glubina application:** fits Anna's "emotional suppression" exactly. Let objects and habits carry Antonov, Polesia, and fear.

### 4. Ordinary people making understandable bad decisions
"One more haul." A drunken yes to a bar night that ends in Tahoe. Not setting the tractor brake. His plots start when a sympathetic person takes a small, human, slightly foolish step.
- **Glubina application:** Timur going into the opening. It should feel like *him*, ambitious, curious, a little reckless, a little broke, not like a plot requirement.

### 5. Storyteller cadence
A conversational, oral-history rhythm: "Story goes…", "Turns out…", colloquial contractions, a narrator who sounds like someone telling you this at a bar. Even his third-person narration (Dust) leans into free indirect voice and borrows the character's idiom.
- **Glubina application:** keep close third *warm*. Narration can take on the POV character's idiom and wry judgment. Avoid a neutral, clinical camera, even in procedural passages.

### 6. Rhythm: long cumulative sentences, then fragments in bursts
Long sentences built with commas, semicolons, and dashes, then two or three short fragments together ("He didn't. He wouldn't."; "Not a dwarven place—older. Too old."). Paragraphs are often long and dense, broken by a short paragraph at an emotional turn.
- **Glubina application:** use this natural variation. Don't let the fragment bursts become the default intensity device. CLAUDE.md bans faked intensity through clipped lines. Fragments should be *rare and earned*.

### 7. Similes from the character's own world
"rattle on my pack like loose teeth"; "like old grudges waiting to be collected"; "the debts started piling like slag heaps." The figurative language comes from the POV character's trade and surroundings, not the narrator's library.
- **Glubina application:** strong rule. Timur's comparisons come from chemistry, lab life, Ufa, cheap equipment, Bashkir home life. Anna's come from marsh, flood channels, gauges. Ben's from ships, machines, the Navy. Zamir's from geological time and folklore.

### 8. Comic dialogue built on people talking past each other
Palace and Borderline: misunderstanding escalates, deadpan meets hysteria, a character persists in the wrong idea. Status games. Exasperation as affection.
- **Glubina application:** the scientists' "weaponized skepticism" and status contests. Banter under stress. Keep it rooted in character, not in quips.

## B. Specific to a piece or genre (don't carry into Glubina by default)

- **Dialect and nonstandard grammar** ("onnit," "turnt," "lettin'"): Durvek's character voice only.
- **Broad, crude, and ethnic humor** (Palace): a comedy-genre and 2014-era register. Not for Glubina.
- **Direct address to the reader** ("you don't need to know about," "yes you know what I'm referring to"): treatment and screenplay convention. Breaks third-person limited.
- **High fantasy register** ("glory on the far side of pain," vows of service): Durvek's genre.
- **Durvek's imagery of stone that hums and bargains made underground**: thematically close to Glubina. **Don't recycle it.** Glubina needs its own images, and echoing Durvek would be self-pastiche.

## D. Dialogue habits to carry forward

- Interruptions, cross-purposes, people refusing the obvious reading.
- Banter as affection between people who know each other.
- Plain, unpolished speech. Nobody speaks in thesis statements.
- Dialogue tags kept simple ("said"). Action beats do the work.
