## 6. Burned-valley evaluation (P-B)

- **Fits geography and timeline:** yes. Southern Urals forest fires are plausible, more so in a climate-strained near future. The tributary would be a downstream outlet of the same karst network.
- **Who encounters it naturally:** not Timur in the prologue (D8 says don't force it). Book 1 candidates: **Anna** (a hydrologist reading flow paths; Ch. 1 already has her looking at the Shulgan's purity), **Zamir** (field geologist; local knowledge of old fires, maybe a folk name for the green strip), or **Leena** (a biochemist, which gives C4 a less miraculous outlet). Either way, it needs a slot in the grid. Author's call.
- **Evidence that would separate it from ordinary recovery:** a paired comparison with an ordinary stream of similar flow through the same burn, showing that moisture alone doesn't explain it. A boundary that follows the anomalous water's path rather than the slope or the soil. Recovery faster than normal post-fire succession for those species. Soil and water chemistry matching the karst outflow signature. **And, critically, change over time.**
- **What it adds beyond the purity anomaly:** it moves the evidence from chemistry to **living systems at landscape scale**, which bridges toward "supports Earth's ecosystem" (D1) without proving it.
- **How it could foreshadow strain (D2):** recovery that's **partial and bounded**: some species and not others; damage the water can't touch; and, most usefully, **a recovery margin that's narrower now than in older imagery or local memory.** That shows limits and decline, and it keeps the system from looking omnipotent.
- **Risk:** reads as "pure water heals plants" if it's shown without the comparisons and the limits.
