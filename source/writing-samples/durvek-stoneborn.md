# Durvek Stoneborn: What Lie Beneath the Stone (FINAL)

> Verbatim from Drive (Jun–Aug 2025). Italic passages are journal entries in the original.

### Durvek Stoneborn

### *Entry One – Rumors Over Ale*

*They say you shouldn't drink on a thin purse or a warm heart. Seems I'm living proof of both.*

*Tonight, I wasn't even halfway through the mug when the bastard said it. Old ruin, out past the slag hills, beyond where sane folk mine anymore. Deep under the Southfall Range. Not a dwarven place—older. Too old.*

*"Stuff be down there, worth more than kings'n crowns, by me word." Barric said.*

*More than kings, sure—but paid for in blood instead of sweat. Even I know that. I have an instinct for things like that. But the fool makes one sense: We are truly out of options. The forge gone cold long ago. The last of the coin went to the broker, trying to cover the cost of protection before those who can fix to take their due with blades instead of parchment. And my brother—my fool, sharp-tongued, too-clever brother—looks at me with that same grin he wore when he bought a crate full of iron shavings. "One more haul, Durvek," he says. "One more job and we can pay it off. Start over." And I gave him back the same face I had when it turnt out the iron shavings were painted twine.*

*And thinking more onnit, I should've said no.*

*But I looked at him, thin from too many skipped meals, hands cut from too many days searching the heaps for scrap, and I said what I always say: "Alright then, let us hear you out."*

I was born in **Stonebreach**, though folk these days just call it Breach. It was a proper hold once, not grand like Kraggodan or Highhelm, but proud enough. Built into the western edge of the Five Kings Mountains, near where the foothills break into scrub and the sky starts smelling more like dry grass than forge-smoke.

Stonebreach lived off salvage, mostly, brought down from the more noble of regions. We didn't source from the rich vein-mines the highlords claimed, but were relegated to the middling kind of ore, the kind where you scraped more dust than metal, but it kept the forges burning and the ledgers inked if barely. We had trade with the outlying human settlements near Druma—wagon caravans bringing worked stone, forged nails, hinges, good dwarven steel when we had it. All of it that could melt went to the forge, to be remade into the distilled sum of so many broken parts. So was the cycle of life in Stonebreach; we saw value in what others discarded, we saw that there could be glory on the far side of pain.

There would come a day where I felt what it was like to be so reforged, but before all that… gods, before that we were *alive*.

Berric and I, we were *stoneborn*—not just in name, but in truth. We'd race through the narrow market halls of Breach, dodging hammer-swingers and smiths with hot tongs like they were slow-witted trolls, laughing like kings. He was smaller, sharper, always faster. I was stronger, steadier, the one they'd trust to carry a barrel or hold a ladder steady on the steeper climbs. When Father worked the forge, we'd sit out front and argue about how many dragons we'd slay or what mountains we'd scale. Father wasn't fond of these flights of fancy. He wanted us to be focused on more practical things; more tactile futures. To be fair, I was no layabout. Father used to say I was slow with the hammer, but sharp with the eye. Once, back when the forge ran hot and the winter winds howled through Breach, the bellows cracked halfway through a contract for wagon axles. I fixed it with tar, scrap canvas, and the tongue-lashing of a blacksmith twice my weight breathing down my neck. Saved a week's worth of work, that did—and maybe kept two dozen folk fed that season. I wasn't much for responsibility, but I knew how to stitch what was broken. Meanwhile Berric would focus on what kind of beard we'd grow when we came of age. He once said he'd dye his green, like polished malachite, just to make the elders sputter their tea. Between the two of us, we were more trouble than the town could manage and more proud than it could break. I used to think we had all the time in the world to be legends. Turns out legends don't always live long enough to matter.

### *Entry Two – Downward*

*We aim to leave before dawn, before the townsfolk can watch two broke fools walk toward their own graves.*

*Packed light while Barric slept: ropes, chisels, the old hammer, the one with the shaft half-rotted. I sharpened it anyway. Would take the climbing gear, but we sold that two winters ago to keep the heat running for an extra month. What's left of it tends to rattle on my pack like loose teeth.*

*The ruin wasn't far, Barric claims—but it hardly could be mistaken for close, either. Just under a week out, past the good rock, into the veins of dead mines and broken tunnels. Not a place for scavengers. Not a place for anyone. I nary expect to see much sign of life that far out, which to a scavenger begins to sound like music. I'm starting to think maybe that little flint rat is on to something good here, for once.*

Back then, amidst the glory of youth, we felt as if the path of fate was laid out as clear as a map on a table. But everything changed the year the *Ashlung Fever* came.

Nobody knows where it started. Some blamed the Druman traders. Others swore it was bad luck brought on by digging too deep into caverns the gods sealed for good reason. All I know is that it came fast—started like a cough, just dry throat from forge dust, or so we thought. Then the fever, red-rimmed eyes, hacking fits that rattled the ribs, and the worst of it—a gray ash, almost like soot, leaking from the lips by the end. Even still, Berric and I felt untouchable, looking ahead to the promise of a life we'd seen so many pride themselves in.

Our priorities were always scattered. We spent so much of that season running trade for the medicine supplies; well, mostly. Berric the selfish fool spent as much effort trying to trade for his own warhammer. Never could much stand the selfish streak in him. Once when old Fargin's supply cart overturned near the northern ridge, he was nowhere to be found. Snow up to my waist and folk said leave it—too far, too late. But I was always the strong one. Hauled near a dozen crates of winter grain back myself, teeth grit and shoulder near tearing off the bone. Nobody said thank you. Didn't need it. It was enough just seeing his kids get through the freeze. If we'd known it was the last winter with Father, we might both have stayed a little closer to home.

My father, **Bardek Stoneborn**, was the town's forge-master then. Finest hammerhand I ever knew. Even when the sickness took his lungs, he kept swinging that hammer until he couldn't lift his arms. The forge was cold three days before his funeral pyre burned, and I'll never forget the sound it made—like a hollow drum, like we'd hammered the last nail into the coffin of Stonebreach itself.

### *Entry Three – Collapse*

*The entrance was a maw, half-choked with fallen shale, but my brother's eyes lit up like a child finding toys beneath stone. "We'll be rich by sunrise," he said.*

*It felt wrong. The walls breathed wrong. The way stone pressed on us like old grudges waiting to be collected. But I followed. I always follow. Should've stayed out. Should've run.*

*The quake hit like a forge hammer, sudden and sharp, the ceiling splintering like rotten wood. The rope snapped. I saw him look back at me, mouth moving, words swallowed by the grind of breaking stone.*

*Then the dust took him. Took everything.*

*I don't know if he screamed. I'll hear it anyway, whether he did or not.*

*Damn him for dragging me out here.*

*Damn me for lettin' him.*

After the loss of Father, things never quite righted. Half the town dead, the other half coughing too hard to swing a pick proper. Livelihood as a concept withered away. Trade dried up. And with no coin coming in, the debts started piling like slag heaps nobody could cart away. Berric felt this loss heavily; but he mourned the loss of *hope* perhaps even a little bit more.

Now? There's nothing left in Stonebreach for me but old debts, dead memories, and a life sour as smoke.

### *Entry Four – The Weight*

*I don't know how long I've been here. Might be a day. Might be three.*

*The ceiling's low, just enough to sit hunched. Breath comes dry. My throat feels like grit in an hourglass. Every creak above me's another promise of stone falling harder.*

*It's not death yet. But it's patient. And it's comin'.*

*I tried callin' out for him. Got no voice left for it now. Can't tell if it's silence or if the mountain's already buried the sound before it could echo.*

### *Entry Five – Dust Made Flesh*

*I don't know what happened... One breath I was coughing up blood and stone dust. The next… I was the dust. I mean it felt like I shattered from the inside out. Like the bones broke before the skin and the flesh flaked off after like chipped clay.*

*And then I woke—outside. Out past the mouth of that ancient ruin, belly down in muddle, hands cracked open like old pottery. There were pulsing, engorged veins running up my arms like bad marble. My whole body feels dry, like I'm carrying cracks beneath the skin waiting to break open with the wrong step. I feel heavy, strong but ancient. Brand new but tired. But by the forge I'm breathing. Somehow, I am breathing.*

**(GM:** ***By now we've established character motivations, backstory, skills in mining/smithing/carpentry/scavenging that could translate to perception and knowledges; we also see a personality centered around cool temperament, caution and devotion. Even a bit of gullibility. We know pet peeves and some noble wins, and have a clear idea of his moral compass; at least as it was when things were clear black and white like cut marble.)***

After the cave-in—after the fool's errand of trying to be something I wasn't—everything changed. Something down there reached up and made a one-sided bargain. And I found no riches buried deep. Instead I lost everything that mattered, and in return, I got this curse. Or blessing. Or whatever foul arrangement the stone struck with me when it decided I shouldn't stay buried.

It gave me life, or what's left of one. And it gave me this… power. When I reach out, I feel the weight of the world beneath my fingertips. The stone listens now—but it's no mercy. It hums beneath the soles of my boots, thrumming with memory older than anything that walks or breathes. I can feel cracks before they form, sense a tremor before it comes. With a thought, I can will stone to move—not out of kindness, but because it *knows* me now, the way a scar knows the blade that made it. I can shape rock like clay, raise it into shields, call it to hold an enemy fast—but every time I do, it's like the mountain whispering: *You belong to me.*

I can heal others, mend what's broken in them, but I can't fix the rot working its way through me. The cracks. The wasting. Piece by piece, I'm coming apart. And that's the joke of it: I spent my life salvaging broken things, and now I'm just another piece of scrap, still walking but patched up by the earth itself.

But I've got work yet to do. Not for coin, though I'll take enough to keep boots on my feet and salt in the stew. No, I'm here because there are things in this world that break, and folk who need mending—and I've spent much too long trying to mend myself just to wind up like this. The world wants me in broken flesh, plain to see, but it cannot do the same for my spirit.

And make no mistake—I'll find what did this to me. I'll find a way to be whole again, to stop being something broken wearing skin like a bad disguise. Until then, I'll stand where others can't. I'll take the blows meant for those softer, smaller, better than me. If I couldn't save him… Barric… then by rights I will save everyone else.

That's what keeps me climbing. One step. One stone. One breath at a time.
