# Dust — Working Title

> Verbatim from Drive (Aug 2024). The first two paragraphs are prose. The rest are the author's story notes.

Stoic, AZ has always been a town that existed apart. Even generations ago, before the Closure, the town existed as an oasis amidst the arid nothingness that constitutes the Sonoran desert. Stoic had been the dream of Josiah Prentiss Burton, a banker from New Orleans who heard the dream of the west calling. Story goes he went as far as the wagon axle would take him and when it broke, he settled into a nearby valley. Just hours away from starvation and desperate of thirst, Josiah found a wellspring nestled in the near dead center of the valley surrounded by a patch of green. This saving grace became the closest thing Stoic had to a holy place, the Wellspring, which exists even today albeit in a more modern facility capable of sustaining the population of nearly 300. Though protected from the worst of the summer dust by the outer valley hills, the town saw drought and hardship one would expect from the desert. The town, most surmised, was named after Burton's steadfast resolve in staking his claim. A resolve that would persist, through his family line and those of the other settlers found there way to Stoic over the years, and would persist still into the unknown future of the Closure.

Initially unsure of what caused it, the town records report "a haze" settling over the outer rim of the valley hills that made the stars and moon seem as if they were being viewed through parchment paper. Grandad always referred to it as if the entire town were still half asleep, seeing the outside world as one sees themselves to the pisser first thing early light. Grandad had a sense of joke like that. I miss that about him. The Closure happened many years ago, and was about as far back in Witte's recall as if he'd been imagining Josiah Burton's journey itself. Witte didn't much care for the past, finding it too full of failure and hardness that if dwelled upon would make him tear up and crawl into bed for the entire day. An outcome he knew from experience.

In the story we should consider it taking place in two time periods, immediately the night of the storm and some years later after people have acclimated to the realization that they are completely on their own. The time dialation between the town and the storm outside perfectly seems to explain why nobody has ever come back... they may simply walk out and back in but by the time they return to the town, a large amount of time has passed and their loved ones have perhaps even moved on without them.

A father goes into the storm night 1, his son follows years later after trying to find an answer, for the father it is the same night he left.

Establishing rules of the world: Time moves faster inside the storm, so from the perspective of those inside when people leave they never come back. When the travellers go outside the storm to our world it is not immediately apparent that anything has changed other than the world has passed. Technology would be essentially analogous between the two, as no large discoveries are likely to be happening in a town of 300-500.

We'll also be telling chapters from POV at first, between the viewpoint of a person inside the storm valley many years after the closure had become an establishing fact of the world and then some the night of the closure itself. It would be cool if it is not at first apparent that these are two different time periods ala West World.
