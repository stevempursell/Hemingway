# Scene Grid — Witte (Treatment)

> Verbatim from Drive (Feb 2012).

Treatment of Witte

Where do I begin. I guess i'd say that Witte is really an extension of myself, though as I write him he becomes less so. I gave away my good names so I figured to start using them soon. Witte isn't the freshest character, and he's lived countless struggles in my mind, but the one i'm finally using him for concerns Cait and Witte and Faith. It's the story of what happenes when Witte, widowed in his early thirties, is rescued from his loneliness by a few choice friends from high school that began to wonder where he got to all those years ago.

We open with Witte, San Francisco, lonely and going through the motions, a conversion strategist. It's a vague job, with vague requirements that you don't need to know about. There's a lot of paperwork, we'll leave it at that. But it pays enough to suit his needs. Witte, in his car, trying to drive. But he's stuck in traffic. And he know's he could take any other road instead of the 45 and make it home earlier. But there's nothing there for him, and he's always felt comfortable on the 45. By the time he gets home he has just enough time to make a dinner, clean up a little and watch a little news before he has to go to bed and head back to the office the next day. Writing from home is a luxury his job could, and once did, lend him. But one Cait passed away he did all he could to stay out of the place. Thyroid cancer, we now know, is not a war that spans years and decades. It sneaks up and steals what's best in your life, turning it cold in an instant. He remembers Cait as he sleeps, she used to always chide him for using more dishes than he needed. It's the same food, she says. It's going the same place, she says. He wishes he could go where she went, go with her. He's washed the same plates seven times a week for as far as memory serves him. Hope she'd be proud of how the house looks.

But again, the place is more a prison these days. It's during the routine come and go from work and life that, waiting for Witte at his house is a face he hasn't seen in years. Aaron waits for him, accusatory of Witte missing his texts and calls. Witte has no apology to make, but mumbles out an I'm Sorry out of obligation. Big news, Aaron is getting married up in Tahoe. Tahoe, too close to Reno, too many memories of growing up. Thanks, but no thanks. So Aaron pushes at least for a celebratory night out at the bar. It's a struggle but he gets a yes from Witte. Cue the blur of a night spent with old friends and Jameson and we fast forward to the next morning, where a bewildered Witte wakes up on the couch of a hotel room in Tahoe. He didn't. He wouldn't. We did: two days to go until the wedding and Witte is captive in a hotel far too close to memories he'd just as soon forget. Roped into a free room, and captive in a snowy hell, Witte makes the best of his stay while avoiding memories he and Cait made here. BLAST from the past, old friends keep shaking him from his safety zone. He can't get a moment to spare to think about her, he's got to go Skiing with Roseline and Jeremy, he's got to have drinks with Aaron and his fiance Becca. He's got to worry about running into Faith, the girl he left behind for Cait, and the girl Aaron remembers him as being perfect for. It's far too soon, it'll always be too soon. It wouldn't be right. So he locks himself in his hotel room for hours.
